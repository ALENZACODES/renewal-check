# Renewal Check

A one-page tool that shows a Canadian homeowner what their mortgage payment will do at renewal, and what happens if they try to keep the old payment.

## The Unique Kernel
Most calculators answer "what is my new payment?" Renewal Check also answers the question people actually face at the lender's kitchen table: "If I keep paying what I pay now, how much longer will this take, and does it even cover the interest?" It puts both paths side by side for a few realistic rates.

## Who It's For
A homeowner in Ontario whose five-year fixed term, signed when rates were low, ends in the next year. Today they wait for the lender's renewal letter, sign it, or call a broker without knowing what to ask.

## The Core Loop
They type four numbers from their mortgage statement and the rate they've been offered. They see the new payment, the payment change, and the "keep my payment" result. They nudge the rate up or down to see a different offer and compare.

## Inspiration and Identity
Calm and plain, like a well-made bank statement. No hype, no sign-up. Works on a phone.

## Why This Matters to the Learner
John has worked in Canadian mortgages for about 20 years. Renewal is where he sees people lose money by signing the first offer.

## What "Working" Looks Like
Enter a $400,000 balance with 20 years left at a 2.29% old rate and a 4.49% offer. The screen shows the new payment, how much more that is per month, and that keeping the old payment would stretch the mortgage by many years. That last line is the moment.

## The POC Boundary
One page. Inputs, results, a rate scenario table, a short list of questions to ask the lender, and a plain note on the 2024 straight-switch rule with its source.

## Later
Saving or sharing a result, prepayment options, variable-rate trigger points.

## Explicitly Cut
Live rate feeds (would need a paid data feed and goes stale), accounts (nothing to protect yet), lender recommendations (advice needs a proper conversation, not a widget).
